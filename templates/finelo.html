What is ORB Trading?
Beginner · 3 min read

The Opening Range Breakout (ORB) strategy is one of the most popular intraday trading strategies. It focuses on the price action during the first 15-30 minutes after the market opens.

Key Concepts

  • Opening Range - The high and low of the first 15-minute candle after market open (9:30-9:45 AM ET)
  • Breakout - When price moves above the high (bullish) or below the low (bearish) of the opening range
  • Entry Signal - Enter a trade in the direction of the breakout
  • Take Profit - Set based on a percentage of the entry price or measured move

Why It Works

The first 15 minutes often set the tone for the trading day. Institutional orders and overnight sentiment create a range that, once broken, often leads to momentum in that direction.

Understanding Support & Resistance
Beginner · 4 min read

Support and resistance levels are key price levels where buying or selling pressure is expected to be significant enough to pause or reverse a trend.

Support Levels

  • Price level where demand is strong enough to prevent further decline
  • Buyers step in at these levels, creating a "floor" for prices
  • Previous lows, round numbers, and moving averages often act as support

Resistance Levels

  • Price level where selling pressure prevents further advance
  • Sellers overwhelm buyers, creating a "ceiling"
  • Previous highs, psychological levels, and trend lines serve as resistance

SMA Crossover Strategy
Intermediate · 5 min read

The Simple Moving Average (SMA) crossover strategy uses two moving averages of different periods to generate buy and sell signals.

How It Works

  • Fast SMA (5-period) - Responds quickly to price changes
  • Slow SMA (20-period) - Smooths out noise, shows overall trend
  • Golden Cross - Fast SMA crosses above Slow SMA = bullish signal
  • Death Cross - Fast SMA crosses below Slow SMA = bearish signal

Best Practices

Wait for confirmation - don't enter on the first tick of a crossover. Look for volume confirmation and ensure the crossover happens near a support/resistance level for higher probability trades.

Risk Management Fundamentals
Intermediate · 6 min read

Risk management is the most important skill in trading. Without it, even the best strategy will eventually lead to significant losses.

Core Principles

  • Position Sizing - Never risk more than 1-2% of your account on a single trade
  • Stop Loss - Always set a stop loss before entering a trade
  • Risk/Reward Ratio - Aim for at least 2:1 reward-to-risk ratio
  • Diversification - Don't put all your capital into one trade or sector

The 1% Rule

With a $100,000 account, you should risk no more than $1,000 per trade. This means if your stop loss is 2% away from your entry, your maximum position size is $50,000 (50 shares of a $1,000 stock).

Candlestick Patterns
Advanced · 7 min read

Candlestick patterns are visual formations created by price action that can signal potential reversals or continuations.

Reversal Patterns

  • Hammer/Hanging Man - Small body with long lower shadow. Hammer is bullish at bottom, Hanging Man is bearish at top
  • Engulfing - Large candle that completely engulfs the previous one. Bullish engulfing at bottom, bearish at top
  • Doji - Open and close are nearly equal, showing indecision. Often signals trend exhaustion
  • Morning/Evening Star - Three-candle pattern that signals major reversals

Continuation Patterns

  • Three White Soldiers - Three consecutive bullish candles with higher closes
  • Rising/Falling Three Methods - Brief consolidation within a trend

Trailing Stop Loss Strategy
Advanced · 4 min read

A trailing stop loss automatically adjusts as the price moves in your favor, locking in profits while still allowing room for the trade to develop.

How Trailing Stops Work

  • For long positions: the stop moves up as price rises, but never moves down
  • For short positions: the stop moves down as price falls, but never moves up
  • Set the trailing distance based on volatility (ATR is commonly used)

Advantages

  • Locks in profits automatically as the trade moves in your favor
  • Removes emotion from exit decisions
  • Allows winners to run while limiting downside

Trading Psychology
Expert · 5 min read

Trading psychology is often the difference between profitable and losing traders. Understanding your emotional responses to market movements is critical.

Common Psychological Traps

  • Fear of Missing Out (FOMO) - Chasing trades after they've already moved significantly
  • Revenge Trading - Taking impulsive trades after a loss to "make it back"
  • Overtrading - Trading too frequently or with too large positions due to overconfidence
  • Loss Aversion - Holding losing positions too long hoping they'll recover

Building Mental Discipline

  • Follow your trading plan religiously
  • Accept that losses are a normal part of trading
  • Keep a trading journal to track emotions and decisions
  • Take breaks after consecutive losses
